Co-Authoring & Parallel Agents

Working with one agent is chat. Working with four parallel agents while editing the same repository demands clear authorship, per-author undo, zero focus theft, and review surfaces that behave like real buffers.

v0.1.0 Spine design guide

This lesson teaches Minga's v0.1.0 co-authoring design (ADR-0006). Today's rolling build already ships revision-checked staged overlays, three-way merge, crash-safe drafts, and syntax-highlighted read-only diff review; see Lesson 01 for today's prerelease keys.

1. Reading The Twist and The Orbit

Every color on screen maps to a semantic theme role (minga, you, needs_you, failure, done), defaulting to Catppuccin Frappé. Two visual marks carry the entire state of agent work:

The Twist (code gutter only)

Authorship

A braided strand in the gutter marks Minga's unreviewed lines and pins its crossing to the top of the viewport as you scroll. Edit inside Minga's block and one strand turns blue (you) on the lines you touched. Uncommitted lines you wrote alone show a thin single you strand; accepted lines fade to a quiet muted strand until commit.

The Orbit (six state words, five shapes)

Status

One canonical BEAM state value renders identically in the modeline, *minga-tasks*, Subagent Peek, and notifications: ◐ Working, ◉ Needs you, ○ Waiting / Stopped, ◆ Failed, and ● Done. Only Needs you escalates, and it never steals cursor focus.

2. Undo is yours; Reject is for Minga

In ordinary editors, letting an agent touch a file you are actively editing scrambles your undo stack. One u wipes out lines you did not write, or undoing an agent step erases your hand-typed fix.

Minga splits undo by author. Pressing u or ⌘Z undoes only your own edits. Minga's changes stay grouped per agent step and leave only when you explicitly Reject them. You have two complementary review workflows:

Mode A: Inline in your file (while you work)

  • ]h / [h Jump to the next or previous Minga change. While the cursor sits inside a change, the modeline shows change 1/2.
  • SPC a y / SPC a d Keep or reject the change at the cursor (or every change touched by a Visual selection such as vah SPC a y). . repeats the last keep or reject; counts and ex ranges (:'<,'>Minga keep, :%Minga reject) work directly.
  • g. or click twist head Open the Act Menu on this change: split y Keep / n Reject decision bar with line consequences on top, plus Show Diff and Open Conversation below. Keeping a change that still has an LSP error asks for confirmation first.

Mode B: Batch review in *minga-review* (SPC a r)

When Minga finishes a multi-file task, press SPC a r to open *minga-review* in a drawer beside your code. Because *minga-review* is a special buffer, bare letters act on each item: y keep, n reject, = toggle inline diff, Y keep all (with y/n confirm), ]h/[h step changes, ↩ jump to the line in its file, and q close. Running :Minga review also populates standard quickfix so ]q and :cdo work across unreviewed changes.

4. Tasks, Subagent Peek, and the Editable Queue

When multiple agents work across files or fan out into subagents, three dedicated buffers keep everything inspectable without adding permanent sidebars:

  • SPC a a
    *minga-tasks*     Every task and subagent tree, needs-you first. Expanded approval rows use uniform keys (y approve once, a session, t turn, n deny, = diff) with a 300 ms anti-misfire lockout if a drawer auto-opens. Press c to start a new task, r to retry waiting/failed rows, o to view retained output (]]/[[ jump failures, f ask Minga to fix), i to inspect, x to stop, and q to close.
  • SPC a i
    Subagent Peek     Inspect a worker in a right split without leaving your parent conversation. Shows the folded Brief (za), live steps, result, direct composer, and a sibling glyph strip stepped in place with ]] / [[. Your unsent draft in the parent conversation stays intact.
  • SPC a v / SPC a q q
    *minga-queue*     Steer immediately or queue follow-ups like code. In the conversation composer, ↩ in Insert adds a newline; Normal-mode ↩ or ⌘↩ sends and steers Minga while it works; ⌥↩ queues a follow-up in *minga-queue* (an ordinary buffer where dd and p reorder upcoming prompts); ⌘. or SPC a s stops. Attach context via SPC a x (SPC a x b buffer, SPC a x f file, SPC a x d diagnostics).
  • SPC a w / SPC a p Watch Minga live, or publish edits. SPC a w opens a follow split showing Minga's edits and *minga-shell*; SPC a p opens one Publish edits… prompt offering publish or discard.

5. Safe quit and voice at the edges

Pressing SPC q q (or macOS Quit) quits immediately when nothing is at stake. If unsaved work or active edits remain, the quit prompt lists exactly what is open and offers explicit keys: s save all and quit, d discard only your unsaved edits and quit (Minga's unreviewed changes stay saved for next time), or esc cancel. Quitting never silently drops Minga's staged work.

Minga keeps its personality quiet and strictly at the edges: "Servus." on welcome, "Passt." once when a task completes, and "Ciao." on quit. Everywhere else, status uses plain operational words.
